Draft the base document22 MIN

Goal: six headed sections, with real content in at least four.

Do this now
  1. Create a new document titled for your role's intelligence base.
  2. Paste your role's section headings from the block below.
  3. Fill four sections minimum with real material. Perfect is not required — real is.
  4. Put a last-reviewed date at the top, and on any section that ages quickly.
◈ HR Scouting Exec — your template
# TALENT INTELLIGENCE BASE — last reviewed [DATE] ## 1. ROLE SCORECARDS Per open role: must-have requirements, nice-to-haves, and the specific evidence that proves each one. Not "strong communicator" — "has written external-facing technical documentation, link required". ## 2. WHAT GOOD LOOKED LIKE Two profiles of people who succeeded here and what predicted it. Two who didn't work out, and what we missed at screening. ## 3. CALIBRATION LANGUAGE What Strong / Possible / No mean at our company specifically, with a real (anonymised) example of each. ## 4. COMPENSATION BANDS By level and location. Marked with the date they were set. ## 5. COMPLIANCE BOUNDARIES Attributes never to be assessed or inferred. Required record-keeping. What we can and cannot say in a rejection. ## 6. SOURCING CHANNELS Which channels produced hires that lasted, and which produced volume without quality.
Prioritise
Sections 1, 2 and 5 — scorecards, past outcomes, compliance.
Section 2 is the one everyone skips and the one that carries the most signal. Failed hires are your best training data.
◉ Content & Social — your template
# CONTENT INTELLIGENCE BASE — last reviewed [DATE] ## 1. VOICE Three adjectives, then a paragraph written in the voice and a paragraph deliberately written wrong. The contrast teaches more than the adjectives. ## 2. PROVEN PATTERNS Our five best-performing posts, verbatim, each with one line on why it worked. Our three worst, with why they failed. ## 3. BANNED LANGUAGE Exact phrases we never use. Superlatives we cannot substantiate. Claims that need legal review before publishing. ## 4. AUDIENCE MAP Who they are, what they already know (so we never explain it), what they are sceptical of, where they actually spend time. ## 5. PLATFORM RULES Per platform: format, length, what our audience responds to there specifically, posting cadence. ## 6. COMPETITOR POSITIONS What each competitor claims, and the space that leaves us.
Prioritise
Sections 1, 2 and 3 — voice, proven patterns, banned language.
Section 3 saves the most time in practice. Preventing the unusable draft is cheaper than editing it.
▣ Project Manager — your template
# DELIVERY INTELLIGENCE BASE — last reviewed [DATE] ## 1. PROGRAMME CHARTER Scope, explicit out-of-scope, success measures, and the constraints that cannot move. ## 2. STAKEHOLDER MAP Who decides what, who only needs informing, what each one actually cares about, and how each prefers to receive information. ## 3. RAG DEFINITIONS What red, amber and green mean here in measurable terms — not vibes. Include the threshold that flips amber to red. ## 4. RISK PATTERNS The five risks that have materialised on past programmes here, their early warning signs, and what actually mitigated them. ## 5. DEPENDENCY REGISTER External parties, their typical response times, and their historical reliability. ## 6. REPORTING CADENCE Who gets what, when, and in what format. Include what previously got ignored and why.
Prioritise
Sections 3, 4 and 5 — RAG definitions, risk patterns, dependencies.
Section 4 is the difference between reporting a problem and predicting it. Past programmes are your only real early-warning system.
✓ You know it worked when: six headings exist, four have real content, and the document is dated.
2

Attach it to the project5 MIN

Goal: make it standing knowledge, not a chat attachment.

Do this now
  1. Upload the document to project knowledge — not into a message.
  2. Add one line to your system instructions naming the document as authoritative.
  3. That conflict-resolution line matters. Without it, general knowledge quietly wins.
Treat [DOCUMENT NAME] as the authoritative standard. When it conflicts with your general knowledge, follow the document.
◈ HR — also add this line
Never assess against criteria absent from the scorecards section.
◉ Content — also add this line
Check every draft against the banned language section before returning it.
▣ PM — also add this line
Set RAG only using the thresholds in section 3.
✓ You know it worked when: the document is in project knowledge and your instructions name it as authoritative.

Connect one live source18 MIN

Goal: one MCP connector delivering current data. One — not three.

How MCP works
Do this now
  1. Choose ONE source you check most often. Only one today.
  2. Open your connector settings and authorise it. Grant read access only for now.
  3. Verify with a specific retrieval question — something you can independently check.
  4. If it fails, check the permission scope first. That's the cause nine times out of ten.
// Verification prompt — run this right after connecting Using the connected source, list the [5 MOST RECENT ITEMS] with their [KEY FIELD] and [DATE]. If you cannot access the source, say so explicitly rather than answering from memory.
◈ HR — connect this: your ATS, or the Drive folder holding live requisitions.
◉ Content — connect this: your analytics source, or the database holding your content calendar.
▣ PM — connect this: your project tracker (Asana, Jira, Linear) or the Drive folder with live status docs.
✓ You know it worked when: it returned real current data you can verify — and it would have told you if it couldn't reach the source.

When It Doesn't Work

Every one of these has a cause you can name and a fix you can apply in under two minutes.

SymptomActual causeThe fix
It ignores the knowledge document The instructions never say the document is authoritative, so general knowledge competes with it. Add the explicit conflict-resolution line naming the document.
The connector returns nothing Permission scope too narrow, or the resource isn't shared with the authorising account. Check scope first, then sharing. Test with the single simplest possible retrieval.
Answers got longer but not better The base is reference material with no opinions in it. Add the "what good looks like" and "what we never do" sections. Standards change output; facts only pad it.

If You Finish Early

Test the freshness discipline — the failure mode nobody notices until it's embarrassing.

◈ Challenge: put a deliberately outdated comp band in the base with an old date. Ask a comp question. Does it flag the age, or quote it as current?
◉ Challenge: add a competitor position that is six months stale. Ask for positioning. Does it caveat the date?
▣ Challenge: add a dependency whose reliability data is a year old. Ask for a risk view. Does it note the data's age?
