The spine of the whole cohort. Every session adds exactly one layer.

What You Are Building, Session by Session

How to teach this: put this slide up in every session and point at the layer you're adding today. Students who see the same map eight times stop asking "where are we?" and start asking better questions.

🔑 RCTFX — Anatomy of a Production Prompt

Session 2's framework, drawn as a machine with five inputs
Figure 5 · The Five Layers

Drop One and You Can Predict the Failure

Each missing layer produces a specific, diagnosable symptom. This is what makes RCTFX a debugging tool, not just a writing template.

LayerAnswers the question Weak versionProduction version
R — RoleWho is Claude for this job? "You are a marketer" "Act as a technical sourcing specialist who has filled this role three times"
C — ContextWhat can't it see? "We're a B2B company" "Last two hires failed at the same point — depth without autonomy"
T — TaskOne verb, one deliverable "Research and write a post" "Draft one LinkedIn post from the findings below"
F — FormatExact output structure "Keep it short and punchy" "Hook ≤12 words. 3 paragraphs, max 2 sentences each. No hashtags."
X — eXamplesWhat good and bad look like "Use our tone of voice" "Good: 'Four of five teams dropped it in 90 days.' Bad: 'Struggling with adoption?'"
The single highest-yield habit: spend most of your time on C. It is the most-skipped layer and the one that changes judgements rather than just adding detail.

📚 Session 3 — The Knowledge Layer

Three places knowledge can live, and why only one of them scales
Figure 6 · Knowledge Placement

Where Does the Fact Come From?

Every fact your AI uses arrives from one of three places. Most people only ever use the worst one.

Figure 7 · Quality Test

Four Properties of a Base That Actually Works

Dumping files in a folder is not a knowledge layer. These four properties separate a useful base from an expensive pile.

Most common S3 failure: "answers got longer but not better." Cause — the base is reference material with no opinions in it. Fix — add the "what good looks like" and "what we never do" sections. Standards change output; facts only pad it.

🔌 How MCP Actually Works

The diagram that stops MCP feeling like magic
Figure 8 · Connector Architecture

Model Context Protocol, End to End

MCP is a standard plug. Claude speaks one protocol; every tool that implements it becomes readable. Permissions live at the connector, not the model — which is why scope is the fix for 9 of 10 failures.

Teach this line: "The model never gets your password. It asks the connector, the connector checks what you allowed, and only permitted data comes back." That sentence resolves most security objections before they're raised.

⏱ Session 5 — Triggers: Who Presses Run?

Everything you have built still waits for you. This is where you hand over the start button.
Figure 11 · Trigger Types

Three Ways Work Starts — and the Exception Path

The exception rule is the part people skip. An automation with no path back to a human is not autonomy, it's abdication.

Session 5 deliverable: one schedule-based automation, one event-based trigger, and a written exception rule that says exactly when the system must stop and ask.

🛡 Session 7 — Guardrails

Speed without control destroys trust
Figure 13 · Three Layers of Control

Where a Guardrail Can Live

You now have a system that reads real data, makes judgements, and acts without you. Control has to exist at three different points — one layer alone is not enough.

The framing that lands: "Everything before Session 7 made your system faster. Session 7 is the only thing that makes it trustworthy — and trust is what lets you actually deploy it at work."
